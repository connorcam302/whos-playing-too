# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary users are Dota 2 players tracking a known group of accounts, their match history, player profiles, records, comparisons, teammates, and collective performance trends. They are usually scanning after games or between sessions, looking for quick answers: who played, how they performed, what changed under filters, and which records or patterns stand out.

## Product Purpose

Whos Playing is a Dota 2 match logger and statistics dashboard. It pulls match data from Steam and OpenDota, stores it in Postgres, and turns it into searchable, filterable player and group analytics. Success means a user can move quickly from a broad overview to a specific player, match, hero, role, patch, or teammate context without losing their place.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, competitive, familiar. The product should feel like a serious stat room for a friend group: practical enough for repeated use, opinionated enough to feel built for Dota, and direct enough that dense data stays readable.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS landing page tropes, oversized decorative hero sections, novelty gamer UI, heavy fantasy ornament, noisy neon treatments, and charts that look impressive but do not answer a useful question. Avoid burying the product behind explanatory copy. Avoid card grids where every panel has the same weight and rhythm.

## Design Principles

1. Data first, decoration second. The interface exists to make match and player evidence easy to compare.
2. Filters are part of the analysis, not an afterthought. Filter state should stay visible, compact, and consistent across related pages.
3. Dense can still be calm. Use tight spacing, clear hierarchy, and familiar table controls instead of spreading every metric into oversized cards.
4. Dota context should be visual. Hero images, role icons, result color, and patch context should carry meaning where they reduce reading.
5. Preserve user momentum. Tab changes, row clicks, modal opens, and pagination should feel local and should not reset context unnecessarily.
6. Action controls should be easy to find. Use sky blue for active filters, pagination, clear/reset controls, and compact mode toggles when they directly change the analysis surface.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Prioritize keyboard reachable controls, clear focus states, adequate contrast on dark surfaces, text alternatives for decorative images, and hover details that do not hide essential information. Color can support meaning for wins, losses, roles, and impact, but text and numeric labels should carry the result as well.
